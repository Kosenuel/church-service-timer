# Changelog

Newest first. Each release links to its full guide in `docs/history/`. Dates are when the release was built.

## Unreleased

- **Flash and Pulse can show long text a few words at a time.** Text too wide for the LED sign no longer has to be shortened for **Flash** or **Pulse**: it is split into the largest groups of whole words that fit the sign, and they are shown one after another, over and over. For example, "PLEASE WRAP UP NOW" may show as "PLEASE", then "WRAP", then "UP NOW".
  - **Flash** flashes each group twice at its usual rhythm (a second in view), then moves on to the next; after the last group there is a slightly longer pause before it starts again. **Pulse** fades each group in and out over two seconds. The LED sign cannot dim, so with Pulse it shows each group steady for about 1.3 seconds with a short gap between groups. The sign plays this by itself, like Flash, and the LED simulator and the editor's preview show it.
  - Stage screens, web screens, a second screen, full screen on the host and the previews step through the same groups at the same time, each group the same size. With **Wrap long text** on, screens show the whole message at once, flashing or pulsing as one block. In a banner the groups show in the band.
  - Up to 60 characters; for longer text the editor suggests **Scroll long text**. A single word too wide for the sign still has to be shortened. In the **Effect** list, Flash and Pulse no longer say "(must fit)" and say "Long text shows a few words at a time".
  - Nothing new is saved in cues, backups or program files: a cue with Flash or Pulse whose text fits shows as before. A cue saved with long Flash or Pulse text cannot be opened by an earlier version, which still needs the text to fit. A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows the whole text flashing or pulsing.
- **The countdown bar sits just under the words.** A cue with **Countdown bar** used to have its bar near the bottom of the screen, far from the words. It now sits just under them and is about as wide as they are, so the two read as one; the words and bar together stay in the middle of the screen. It is a little thinner than before on large screens (about 2% of the screen's height, 4 to 10 pixels). This is the same on stage screens, web screens, a second screen, full screen on the host and the previews, with **Wrap long text** (under the wrapped lines), in a banner (inside the band) and on a coloured cue (above the band of colour). A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows the bar where it did.
- **Coloured cues on screens have their words in the colour.** A Red, Green or theme-colour cue that fills a stage screen, web screen, second screen or full screen on the host now has its words in that colour, on the same warm charcoal with the band of its colour along the bottom edge. Amber cues keep their warm white words. While Screen flash washes the screen in the cue's colour the words are warm white, then they settle back to the colour. Where a theme's colour is too dark to read clearly on the charcoal, it is lightened a little, just enough, on screens; banners in that colour use the same lighter shade. White cues and banners otherwise look as they did, and a paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows warm white words.
- **Smoother scrolling cues on screens.** A scrolling cue could stutter on a second monitor and on web screens, and sometimes on the Simulator. It now moves on every frame at a steady pace, on paired stage screens, web screens, a second screen, full screen on the host and the stage simulator, and only the moving text is redrawn. Each new update from the host no longer makes the text jump back to the start: it carries on from where it was unless the cue itself changes, including when the speed, colour or overtime changes, or a phone's browser bar hides. The LED simulator still moves a whole column at a time, like the sign, but each column now comes exactly when it is due instead of waiting for the app's next clock tick, so it no longer moves one column and then two. The LED sign itself is unchanged.
- **Full screen on a phone or tablet turns it sideways.** On Android, Full screen on Live now turns the stage picture to landscape, either way round, so the countdown is as large as it can be. When you leave full screen (with **Exit full screen**, Back, or by itself when the service ends) the phone or tablet goes back to turning as it did before. A computer is unchanged.
- **A calmer look for coloured cues on screens.** A cue in Amber, Red, Green or your theme's colour that fills a stage screen, web screen, second screen or full screen on the host is now warm white on a warm charcoal screen, with a band of its colour along the bottom edge, instead of every letter in the colour on black. Pulse, flashing and sliding in move the words while the band stays put; Screen flash washes the screen with a deep tint of the colour before settling; the countdown bar fills in the cue's colour. White cues and banners look as they did, and so does every cue on a paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version.
- **Wrap long text on screens, and only the settings for the preview you are looking at.** Editing a stage cue or a warning's text now shows the settings for the display its preview shows. On **LED sign** there is a new group, **On the LED sign**, with the display font, **Tall · 14 px**, the alignment and the speed on the LED sign. On **Stage screen** there is the **On screens** group instead. The text, **Bold**, **Italic** and **Effect** change both and are always there, and a line under the switch says which settings are showing. Nothing is lost by switching: the hidden settings are kept and saved. The end screen, the zero screen, templates and Send message have no switch and look as before.
  - **Wrap long text.** A new switch under **On screens**. Stage screens, web screens, a second screen, full screen on the host and the previews then show the cue on up to 3 lines (2 in a banner), centred and as large as fits, instead of scrolling it or shrinking it onto one line. A scrolling cue holds still on screens while the LED sign keeps scrolling it. Text that would have to be too small to read on 3 lines scrolls instead, so nothing is cut off. With more than 60 characters, the editor says that long text is hard to read from a distance; you can still save it.
  - Cues, backups and program files you already have look the same as before. A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows the cue as it did. A backup or program file containing a cue with **Wrap long text** needs this version or later to open.
- **Five new effects for cues.** The **Effect** choice in the cue editor now lists, each with a line saying what it does, and the ones whose text must fit the LED sign say so in brackets, as in **Static (must fit)**. The message for text too wide for the sign now names **Scroll long text**:
  - **Flash then hold** flashes three times, at the same rhythm as Flash, then stays steady. The LED sign does this too; while the cue stays up, the sign flashes three times again every 5 minutes.
  - **Pulse** gently brightens and dims, about every two seconds.
  - **Slide in** slides in from the bottom edge of the screen as it appears (a banner slides in from its own edge), then holds still.
  - **Countdown bar** puts a thin bar in the cue's colour under it, which empties as its time runs out: a warning's time, or the time chosen when sending a cue for a set time. A cue shown until cleared has no bar.
  - **Screen flash** washes the whole screen once with a deep shade of the cue's colour (red for a white cue) as it appears, with the cue still in its own colour, then the cue sits on black. In a banner only the band flashes.
  - Pulse, Slide in, Countdown bar and Screen flash are for stage screens, web screens, a second screen and full screen on the host; the LED sign cannot draw them, so it shows the cue steady, and the editor says so. Like Static and Flash, the text must fit the sign. With **Reduced motion** on, a cue that slides in simply appears and Screen flash gives the screen a coloured edge instead of a flash. The countdown under a banner never moves or changes with any of them.
  - Cues, backups and program files you already have are unchanged. A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows the new effects as steady text. A backup or program file containing a cue with one of the new effects needs this version or later to open.
- **The previews beside Stage cues and Warnings & alerts follow your display.** In a wide window, the pane beside Settings › Stage cues and beside Warnings & alerts used to show everything as the LED sign draws it. It now shows them as your display will: **On the LED sign** with an LED sign or the LED simulator, and **On the stage screen** with stage screens or the stage simulator, including each cue's colour and banner. Point at a scrolling cue to play it. Editing a warning's text has the same **LED sign | Stage screen** switch over its preview as editing a cue.
- **Choose what the Simulator simulates.** With no LED sign and no stage screens in use, the Display preview on Live has always shown a pretend LED sign. It can now show a pretend stage screen instead: the large countdown, cues, clock and blank screen as a phone, tablet, web or second screen would show them. Click or tap the chip beside **Display preview**, or use **Change display**, where the Simulator now has **Simulate: LED sign | Stage screen**. The chip there and the one at the top of the window read **LED simulator** or **Stage simulator**. Nothing is asked when you switch; the choice is kept on that device and is not part of backups. An LED sign is still what a new install simulates, a second screen that is showing still takes the Simulator's place in the preview, and locked controls cannot change it. The editor for a stage cue, from **Manage cues** or from a cue on Live, also has a small switch over its preview, **LED sign | Stage screen**, to see the cue as each will show it. It starts on the one you are using, and changing it only changes the preview.
- **Colour and a banner for cues on screens.** The cue editor has a new group, **On screens**, for stage screens, web screens, a second screen and full screen on the host. The LED sign is not changed by any of it.
  - **Colour.** White, as before, or Amber, Red, Green or your theme's colour. They are fixed colours chosen to read clearly on the black stage background.
  - **Position.** **Full screen**, as before, where the cue takes the place of the countdown; or **Banner at the top** or **Banner at the bottom**, where the cue shows in a band and the countdown (or the clock) stays in view. The countdown keeps its place in the middle when a banner appears. A banner needs a running service to sit over, so after the service has ended the cue fills the screen.
  - The speed on screens is in the same group. Bold, italic, flashing and scrolling work as they did, in a banner too.
  - Cues, backups and program files you already have look the same as before. A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows every cue in white and full screen. A backup or program file containing a cue with a colour or a banner needs this version or later to open.
- **A cue can scroll faster on screens than on the LED sign.** A scrolling cue used to move at one speed everywhere, and a speed that suits the sign is slow on a big stage screen. The cue editor now has two sliders: **Speed on the LED sign**, as before, and **Speed on screens**, for stage screens, web screens, a second screen and full screen on the host. The speed on screens goes up to 48, twice the sign's fastest. Until you move it, screens follow the sign exactly as they did, and **Same as the LED sign** puts it back. Cues, backups and program files you already have are unchanged. A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version keeps using the sign's speed. A backup or program file containing a cue with its own screen speed needs this version or later to open.
- **See the second screen on Live.** On a PC, while the stage is showing on a projector or second monitor, the Display preview on Live now has a small live copy of it, in that screen's shape and with the layout chosen for it. With only the Simulator, the copy takes the Simulator's place and the chip beside **Display preview** reads **Second screen**. With an LED sign, or with phone, tablet or web stage screens, their preview stays and the copy sits under it with its own **Second screen** heading; under an LED sign it is the same size as the sign's preview. Press **Stop**, press Esc on that screen or unplug it, and the preview goes back to how it was. Phones and tablets have no second screen, so nothing changes there.
- **Live makes better use of a large screen.** On a big monitor the Live page used to keep the countdown ring small and leave the rest of the window empty. A 1366 × 768 laptop window, and phones and tablets, look the same as before; the Keys layout is unchanged.
  - **Three panes and The dial.** From about a 1920 × 1080 window the ring grows to fill the height, with the buttons straight under it. In a wider window, such as 2560 × 1440, the ring sits at the left with the segment name, Pause, Next segment, the time adjustments, the private note and End service in a column at its right.
  - **Mission control.** In a tall window the timer, the display preview and the cues fill the height above the timeline, with a larger ring and up to 24 lines of activity. Pause and Next segment keep a sensible width. The timeline is taller and each block adds the speaker, the planned time and how long the segment took or when it should start. A block too narrow for that shows less, so nothing is cut off mid-word.
  - **Classic.** In a window about 1840 wide or more, the rundown has a column of its own beside the timer and the display, so the whole service is in view without scrolling.
  - The warning line and a long overtime figure stay inside a large ring, and a long segment name no longer pushes End service off the bottom.
- **Mission control is the layout a new install starts in.** In a wide window, a fresh install and **Reset** now give Mission control with Midnight Lime, and it is listed first in Settings › Desktop layout. If you already use the app, your layout stays as it is; choose Mission control there if you want it.
- **Click the service name to go back to Live.** On Programs, Reports or Settings, the name of the loaded or running service at the top left returns to Live. With no service loaded it is the app's name and does nothing.
- **Mission control with large text.** With Windows or Android set to larger text, the names in the rundown timeline were cut off at the bottom. The timeline now grows to fit.
- **A button for the mini timer.** The mini timer is the small window that stays above your lyrics or slides program, with the countdown, Pause, Next and a cue. It could only be opened from the ⋮ menu or with Ctrl+M, so it was easy never to find. On a PC, while a service runs, Live now has a button for it at the top right, beside Lock controls.
- **A clearer example for review notes.** The example under Review notes on a report now reads “Reduce the Announcements time from next week”.
- **A calendar for the reports date filter.** On a PC, **Dates** on Reports used to open a full-screen list of months scrolling down the middle of the window. In a wide window it is now a small calendar: two months side by side, with arrows to move between months. Click the first day, then the last; the days between are filled in, and the range is written out in words above **Apply**. Beside the calendar are ready-made ranges: Last 4 weeks, This month, Last month, This year, and All dates, which clears the filter. A dot marks each day that has a saved report. The arrow keys move between days, Enter chooses, Page Up and Page Down change the month, and Esc cancels. A phone or a narrow window keeps the picker it had.
- **Full screen on the device running the service.** When your own screen is the one people watch, Live can now show the stage picture on it: the big countdown a second screen or a paired stage screen shows, on black, with warnings and cues as they appear on stage. During a service or a quick timer, use the Full screen button at the top right of Live, **⋮ › Full screen**, Commands, or **F11** on a computer. To leave, press Esc or F11, or tap or click anywhere and then **Exit full screen**; it also leaves by itself when the service ends. The keys work as on Live, and every timer action still asks first, in a small card over the stage. With the controls locked, the keys do nothing and the control offers Hold to unlock. It shows the timer only, the timer and segment name, or the timer and next segment: a computer uses the choice made for its second screen, and a phone or tablet has its own under Settings › Screens & devices, kept on that device and not in backups.

## 0.33.0, 19/November/2026

- **Second screen shows the stage properly.** On a PC, **Show** in Settings › Screens & devices › Second screen could leave the projector or second monitor stuck on a black picture with part of an enormous letter at one edge. It happened when the two screens use different scaling in Windows (for example a laptop at 150% and a 4K monitor at 200%): Windows resized the stage window as it moved across, and the window then stopped updating. The stage window is now created on its own screen from the start and keeps to that screen's exact size.
- **The window opens properly on a second screen.** On a PC with two screens that use different scaling in Windows, the app could start with its loading page filling the window and the app itself drawn small in the bottom-left corner. The window was being moved back to where it was last time after the app had started drawing, and Windows resized it on the way. It now opens at its last place from the start. A window that had grown larger than its screen because of this is brought back inside it.
- **The stage countdown holds still.** On a stage screen, a second screen or a web screen's app view, the large time used to shift and change size slightly each second, because some digits are wider than others and the time was refitted to the screen every time it changed. The time now keeps one size and each digit keeps its place.
- **Drop a program file on the window.** On a PC, drag one program file (`.json`) or spreadsheet (`.csv`) from a folder onto the app's window to import it. It opens in the same review as **Import**, and nothing is saved until you confirm. Anything else is refused, and so is a drop while a service is running or controls are locked.
- **Print from the Live home.** In a wide window, the order of service shown before a service has a small **Print** button beside the program's summary. It opens the same Print order of service choices as elsewhere.
- **Check for updates.** Settings › About has a new row, **Check for updates**. Press it and the app asks GitHub whether a newer version has been published, then offers a button to the download page if there is one. It checks only when you press the row, never on its own, and sends nothing about you or your church.
- **The countdown beside the Windows clock.** On a PC, while a service or quick timer runs, the app adds its icon to the notification area. Point at the icon to read the time, for example "Sermon · 12:34 left", "Sermon · 01:10 over" or "Paused · Sermon · 12:34", while the lyrics or slides program is in front; click it to bring the app's window forward. The icon only shows the time and cannot change the service, and it goes when the service ends. Windows 11 puts new icons behind the small arrow beside the clock: drag the icon out onto the taskbar to keep it in view. Turn it off in **Settings › Warnings & alerts › Show the countdown in the notification area**; the choice is kept on that computer.
- **Keyboard shortcuts and a clicker test.** On a computer, **?** or **F1** opens a list of every key and what it does, grouped as During a service, Stage and Moving around; it is also under **Keyboard shortcuts** in Commands, the Live ⋮ menu and Settings search. It does not open while the controls are locked, and a question mark typed into a field is still a question mark. **Settings › Warnings & alerts › Test a clicker or pedal** is new on phones, tablets and computers: press each button on a clicker or foot pedal and the page names the key it sends (“Page Down”, “Right arrow”, “F5”) and says what that key does on Live, or that the app does not use it. Nothing pressed on that page reaches a running service. No key does anything different from before.
- **The second screen is on Live.** On a PC, showing the stage on a projector or second monitor no longer means a trip into Settings.
  - **One button on Live.** Under the stage preview, in every layout, a line says what the second screen is doing ("Second screen: not showing", or "Showing on Screen 2 · 3840 × 2160") beside one button: **Show on second screen** or **Stop**. It is in the command palette (Ctrl+K) too. With more than one extra screen you choose which; with none it only says "No second screen connected". Lock controls disables it.
  - **It remembers the screen.** The screen you last showed the stage on is kept on that computer and offered first next time.
  - **Open it when a service starts.** Turn on **Settings › Screens & devices › Second screen › Open the second screen when a service starts** and starting a service or a Quick timer shows the stage on that screen. If the screen is not connected, nothing opens and Live says so; it never opens on your own screen by itself. It is off to begin with. Ending the service leaves the end screen showing.
  - **Choose what it shows.** Timer only, Timer and segment name, or Timer and next segment, in the same card. Paired stage screens keep their own choice.

## 0.32.0, 19/November/2026

- **Pages use the room in a wide window.** On a computer, or a large tablet held sideways, pages no longer sit as a narrow phone column in the middle of the screen. Phones and smaller windows look exactly as before.
  - **Live, before a service.** Choose a program on the left and its order of service shows in the middle, under the time of day, with one **Start service** button. Quick timer, Share to screens and Join a service sit underneath. The stage preview and cues are open on the right.
  - **Settings.** The groups sit in two columns. Appearance keeps its preview beside the themes and layouts. Display settings lists its sections down the left. Warnings & alerts and Stage cues show what the sign will show; point at a preview to play it. Church profile, Backup & restore, Feedback centre, Settings PIN, About, Diagnostics and Ready for service each put a preview or a summary beside the settings.
  - **Screens & devices and Join a service.** Screens & devices has three areas: phones and tablets with the app, this computer, and browsers without the app. Join a service is three numbered steps: choose the host, name this device, enter the code.
  - **Reports.** In a large window, review notes and the activity timeline sit beside the timings, and the Graph tab puts its summary beside the charts.
  - **Programs.** The program editor shows the running order beside the form: when each segment starts and when the service ends.
  - **Wording.** On a computer the app says “click” where a phone says “tap”.
  - **Dialogs.** Quick timer, Print, Export, Version history, Segment library, Recover time and Edit upcoming open as dialogs in the middle of the window, not as sheets across the bottom.

- **Warnings on a PC are silent again.** A warning's Windows notification played the Windows notification sound, although the app asked for none: the notification package ignored the request on Windows. The app now carries a corrected copy, so a warning shows its notification and flashes the taskbar button without a sound. If you do want the sound, turn on **Settings › Warnings & alerts › Play the Windows notification sound**. It is off to begin with and is kept on that computer.

## 0.31.1, 19/November/2026

- **Desktop layout is easier to find.** It was the second tab of Appearance, which was easy to miss. Settings now has its own **Desktop layout** row, which names the layout in use and opens straight on it; searching Settings for "layout" finds it; and the Theme tab has a **Desktop layout and spacing** button that switches to the other tab.

## 0.31.0, 19/November/2026

- **A Windows version (first build, still to be tried on a real PC).** Church Service Timer can now be built for Windows 10 and 11, so a service can be run from a laptop or PC. It is the same app: the same programs, reports, themes and settings, and a backup made on the phone restores on the PC and the other way round. On a PC:
  - **Wide layout.** The window can be resized, and it reopens where you left it. In a wide window Live uses the new **Three panes** desktop layout, described below.
  - **Keyboard.** On Live, **Space** asks to pause or resume and a second **Space** or **Enter** confirms, **Page Down** (pressed twice) starts the next segment, **+** or **−** opens Adjust time and **Ctrl+Z** offers to undo the last segment change. Each still asks before it changes anything, and **Lock controls** blocks them all.
  - **It stays awake.** While a service runs, or screens are shared, the PC will not go to sleep or turn its screen off. If it does sleep (a closed lid), the timer is still right when it wakes. Closing the window during a service asks first.
  - **Screens and the LED sign.** The PC can host stage screens and web screens, join another device's service as a stage screen, and drive the W2 LED sign. It can be on a network cable and on the sign's Wi-Fi at the same time. Join the sign's Wi-Fi in Windows' own Wi-Fi settings; the app does not switch networks for you.
  - **Second screen.** With a projector or second monitor plugged into the PC, **Settings › Screens & devices › Second screen** shows the stage output on it full screen: the timer, cues, messages and blanking, exactly as a stage screen shows them. It needs no network or pairing and works whichever display you have chosen. Set Windows to **Extend** (Windows + P) first. **Stop**, or Esc on that screen, closes it.
  - **Files.** Import, export, backup and reports use the usual Windows Open and Save windows. Where the phone says Share, the PC saves the file where you choose.
  - **Warnings are silent.** At each warning a Windows notification appears and the app's taskbar button flashes. It makes no sound, because a PC is often plugged into the sound system. Windows holds notifications back while **Do not disturb** is on, and turns that on by itself when the screen is duplicated to a projector; the taskbar button still flashes.
  - **Not on Windows:** scanning a QR code with a camera (join with the six-digit code instead) and the phone's battery settings.

- **Desktop layouts.** In a wide window (a computer, or a large tablet held sideways) Live now fills the screen instead of scrolling one column of cards:
  - **Three panes.** The whole rundown is on the left, with how long each finished segment really took and how far ahead or behind the service is running. The timer and its controls are in the middle, with the private note in view. The stage preview, cues and messages are on the right. Live, Programs, Reports and Settings become tabs in the header. Everything works as before: every timer action still asks first, and **Lock controls** still blocks them all.
  - **Choose it in Settings › Appearance › Layout & accessibility › Desktop layout.** **Three panes** is the default; **Classic** is the single column of cards from before. Choosing a layout also sets the theme it comes with (Three panes comes with Midnight Lime), and you can then pick any theme you like. If you already chose a theme, updating keeps it.
  - **Saved looks.** **Save this look** keeps the layout, theme, spacing, contrast and key labels together under a name, so you can go back to it with one tap. Up to 8, and they are included in backups. **Reset to default** returns to Three panes with Midnight Lime.
  - **Keys on the buttons.** On a computer, Pause, Next segment and Custom show the key that presses them (Space, Page Down, + and −). Turn this on or off with **Show keyboard keys on buttons**.
  - **Mission control.** A second desktop layout, for seeing the whole service at a glance. A strip across the top says how far ahead or behind the service is, the time now, the planned finish and when it is now set to end, with End service beside it. The timer, the stage preview and the cues sit side by side, and the rundown runs along the bottom as a timeline: each segment is as wide as the time it takes, finished segments show how long they really took with the part that ran over marked, and a line shows where the service is now. Tap a segment for its details. On a computer the keys that work on Live are listed along the bottom. It keeps the side rail and comes with Midnight Lime. In a tall window, such as a full HD screen, it also lists the screens that have joined under the stage preview and the service's latest activity under the cues.
  - **The dial.** A third desktop layout, built around the app's own mark. The whole service is a ring, a piece for each segment, running round to end at the cross; finished segments fill in, with any overrun marked, and a line shows where the service is now. Inside it a second ring shows the time left in the running segment, with the countdown in the middle and the time the service is now set to end beside the cross. The rundown stays on the left and the stage preview and cues on the right, as in Three panes. It comes with Royal Navy.
  - **Keys.** A fourth desktop layout, dense and made to be run from the keyboard. The running segment is one strip across the top: its name, a large countdown, how far ahead or behind the service is and when it will end, and the timer controls. Below it the rundown is a table with a column each for speaker, planned time, start, actual time, how far over or under, and state, with the private note underneath. The stage preview and cues sit beside the table and the keys are listed along the bottom. It uses Compact spacing and comes with Graphite. Before the first segment starts it shows the three panes, which have room for the start controls.
  - **Number keys send cues.** On a computer, in every layout, **1** to **9** send the first nine prepared cues, the same as clicking them; pressing the number of the cue that is showing goes back to the timer. Each cue button shows its number when key labels are on. Like the other keys, they do nothing while typing, on another tab, under a dialog or when **Lock controls** is on.
  - **Commands (Ctrl+K).** On a computer, press **Ctrl+K**, or choose **Commands** in the ⋮ menu on Live, to open a list of everything you can do right now. Start typing, for example "pause", "wrap up" or "settings", use the arrow keys and press Enter. What is most likely needed is suggested first, such as Recover time when the service is running behind. Anything that changes the timer still asks you to confirm, exactly as its button does.
  - **More keys on Live.** **M** sends a message, **E** edits upcoming segments, **R** recovers time, **J** jumps to a later segment, and **Ctrl+L** locks the controls (unlocking is still Hold to unlock). The keys are shown on their buttons and along the bottom of the Mission control and Keys layouts. None of them work while the controls are locked.
  - **Programs use the whole window.** In a desktop layout your programs are listed on the left. Choose one and its segments appear in the middle as a table, with the time, speaker and private note for each. On the right is a summary: the Load into Live button, the shape of the service, its total time, what past services say about the timings, the end screen and the warnings. To change a program, click Edit program or double-click a segment; this opens the same editor as before. Changes never touch a service that is already running.
  - **Reports use the whole window.** In a desktop layout your saved services are listed down the left, and the one you choose opens beside them with its planned and actual time, how far over or under it ran, and a picture of the service: a planned row above an actual row, or a ring ending at the cross if you use The dial. Below it, a table shows every segment with its planned time, actual time and a small bar showing whether it ran under or over. Review notes, Print report and export are all still there. The newest report shows until you choose another.
  - **Finishing touches to the desktop layouts.** The cue buttons now show each cue in the same lettering the stage sees. The dial has an **Up next** box above the rundown with the next item, its length, who is leading it and roughly when it starts, and you can click any part of the ring for that item's details. - **Web screens.** While sharing, any browser on the same Wi-Fi can follow the service without the app ([docs/WEB-SCREENS.md](docs/WEB-SCREENS.md)). **Order of service** shows the rundown and what is happening now, with no times, using a 4-digit code that stays the same each week (or no code, if you choose). **Stage display** (full-screen countdown and messages) and **Service overview** (time left, ahead or behind, finish time) need the 6-digit team code, which changes each time you share and also unlocks switching between all three. Browsers can't control the service or see private notes. Open them with a short easy name made from the church name (for example `grace-community.local`), the QR code or the address. **Export join slide** makes a 1920 × 1080 image to project.
- **Send a program to a paired phone.** While sharing to screens, Program details → ⋮ → **Send to paired phone** sends the program to the connected operator and viewer phones you tick (stage screens are left out). Private notes are optional. The other phone shows a “Program received” card to review the rundown. If it already has a program with that name, **Update mine** replaces it and keeps its current version in Version history, or **Save as copy** adds it with a numbered name. Received programs wait until reviewed, even after leaving the pairing or closing the app (up to five; sending the same name again replaces the waiting one), and also show at the top of Programs. They are not included in backups.
- **Version history.** Each time you save a change to a program, the version before it is kept, up to the last 10. Program details → ⋮ → Version history lists them with what changed (for example “Added “Offering”, +4 min in total”). Tap one to see its rundown and restore it, with Undo. Restoring keeps the current name, and the version it replaces stays in history. History goes with a deleted program (and comes back with Undo) and is included in backups. A backup with history uses backup format 9; backups without it stay readable by earlier versions.
- **Segment library.** Save segments you use often, such as Offering or Announcements, with their duration, speaker, notes and protection. In the program editor, open a segment and tap **Save to library**. **From library** next to Add segment inserts one into any program, with search. Saving the same name again updates it. Programs → ⋮ → **Segment library** lists them and removes one, with Undo. Up to 50 are kept, and they are included in backups (backup format 9).
- **Import any spreadsheet.** A CSV with its own column names (for example `Item, Time (mins), Who`) now opens **Match spreadsheet columns** instead of an error. It shows the sheet, guesses which column is the name, duration, speaker, notes or program, and reads durations written as `5`, `5.5`, `05:30`, `5 min` or `90s`. A live preview lists every problem by row number. The choice is remembered, so next week's sheet in the same layout needs one tap. The result goes to the usual import review.
- **Share by Bluetooth, messaging or email.** The selection bar on Programs and Reports has **Share**, which opens Android's share sheet with a JSON or CSV file, or one PDF sheet per selected item. Print Service also has **Share PDF**. The private-notes and review-note choices work as they do for Export.
- **Open received files.** A program `.json` or `.csv` opened with, or shared to, the app goes to the usual import review. Reports and backups explain where they belong instead of failing to import.
- **Change program in Ready.** Ready now shows the loaded program with a **Change** button. Pick another program (pinned first, with search) and it replaces the loaded one. The pre-service start time and service mode stay, and the sign's countdown is unchanged. An 8-second Undo switches back. Load into Live from Program details does the same while in Ready. The start chooser marks pinned programs with a pin.
- **Programs list:** a pin icon marks pinned programs (the star toggle and each row's ⋮ menu are gone). Long-press a program to start selecting; the selection bar exports, pins or unpins, and deletes the selected programs. The Android Back key leaves selection mode.
- **Reports list:** each row's ⋮ menu is gone too; long-press a report to start selecting, then export or delete from the selection bar. Single reports still have Export, Print and Delete on their report page.
- **Live:** the Android Back key on a loaded service that has not started cancels the setup and returns to Standing by.
- **Back to exit.** Back on Programs, Reports or Settings returns to Live. On Live, it asks you to press Back again before leaving the app. During a service, it asks for confirmation and explains that the timer keeps running.
- **Program IDs.** Each saved program now has a permanent ID on this phone, so renaming one keeps its pin, recent use, unsaved edits and the review-note link from its past services. Existing programs get IDs automatically on first launch. Shared files, exports and backups leave the ID out, so earlier versions still open them, and a received program always gets its own. This is groundwork for version history and sending programs between phones.
- **Diagnostics.** A new row at the bottom of Settings → About. **Record service metrics** records the next service, then switches itself off. Once a minute and at key moments it notes memory, battery level and temperature, screen smoothness, timer accuracy, how promptly warnings fire, backgrounding and reconnections. When the service ends the phone writes a report with ✅ or ❌ against each target (smoothness, memory growth, timer drift, warnings, reconnects, and battery against this phone’s previous recording), shown on the Diagnostics page. It never records service content or people, and nothing leaves the phone until **Share recording**, which sends the report as a markdown file (footer “Created by I-Proton”) with the minute-by-minute CSV. While recording is on, Live shows a **Recording metrics** chip. **Start 2-hour test service** runs a generated service with recording on and saves no report.
- **Performance review** before each release: scrolling benchmarks, a 2-hour soak script for a USB-connected phone, and a report with pass marks ([docs/PERFORMANCE-REVIEW.md](docs/PERFORMANCE-REVIEW.md)).
- **Updating:** this version upgrades saved data on first launch (program IDs), so the phone cannot go back to 0.25 afterwards; export a backup first if you might. Backups that contain version history or a segment library use backup format 9, which 0.25 cannot open; other backups stay readable by 0.25.
