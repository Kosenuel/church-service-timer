# Changelog

Newest first. Each release links to its full guide in `docs/history/`. Dates are when the release was built. From 0.35.0, version numbers follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org) (MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH); see [Versioning](docs/BUILD.md#versioning) for how each release's number is chosen.

## Unreleased

- **Song words, Bible verses and announcements on the screens.** A new **Slides** page, after Programs in the navigation, and a **Slides** button on Live under the display preview, put words on the second screen, web screens and stage screens.
  - **Library** on the left keeps every song, set of Bible verses and announcement you save, for every service, most recently used first. Search finds a title or any words in it. **Add a song**: type or paste the words, or **Open a text file**. A blank line starts a new slide, and a line such as Verse 1, Chorus, Bridge, Refrain, Pre-chorus, Intro, Outro, Tag or Ending names the slide and is not shown. **Lines on a slide** keeps the words as typed or splits them into slides of 2 or 4 lines; the slides it makes show beside the words as you type. **Save song**. Edit or delete (it asks first) from the ⋮ beside each one.
  - **Bible verses**: type a reference such as John 3:16, John 3:16-18, Psalm 23 or 1 Cor 13:4-7 (common short names work) and press **Find**. The King James Version comes with the app; each slide shows a few verses as large as they fit, with the reference in gold under them ("John 3:16 · KJV"). A reference that does not exist says what is wrong ("There is no chapter 40 in John: it has 21 chapters."). Other translations, such as the NLT, are under copyright and cannot come with the app: **Paste your own** takes the words from any translation your church may show, with its own reference and translation name.
  - **Announcements**: a title, the words, and a date, time or place line, laid out as a slide: the title large in gold, the words under it.
  - Click or tap a slide to put it on the screens: the slide on the screens is ringed in red and the next one is dashed. On a computer, **Right arrow** or **Space** goes to the next slide, **Left arrow** back, **Esc** clears the slide and **B** turns the screens black (again to bring the slide back). These keys work on the Slides page only, so on Live, Space and the arrows still pause and move to the next segment as before. **On the screens now** shows what the screens show, with Previous, Next, Clear slide and Black. On a phone or tablet the page is one column, with those four buttons along the bottom.
  - **Send to**: Second screen, Web screens and Stage screens, each a tick box. Stage screens are not ticked to begin with, so a speaker's countdown is never covered by accident. Full screen on this device and the stage simulator show what the second screen shows. The LED sign never shows slides. **Clear slide** gives every screen back exactly what it showed before: the countdown, the clock, a cue or the end screen. A quick message to the stage still shows over a slide.
  - The look: twelve drawn backgrounds (Black, Midnight, Aurora, Sunrise, Mountain dusk, Starry night, Candlelight, Ocean, Forest mist, Stained glass, Royal velvet, Linen), 23 public-domain and CC0 photos at full HD, under Nature and People (listed under **Photo credits**), or **Your picture**; **Dim** (70% to begin with, up to 90%); text size S, M or L; Centre or Left; **Motion** Still or Gentle (a slow zoom, drift, shimmer, twinkling stars or rising embers; never with Reduced motion); and **Countdown in the corner**, a small countdown on the slide while a service runs. Words are as large as fit, never cut off, white with a soft shadow, or dark on Linen. Your picture shows on the second screen, full screen and web screens; phones and tablets showing the stage get Midnight in its place for now.
  - **Fits the rest of the app.** The Slides page and its editors take the finish you chose in Settings (the page is cards like Live and Settings; the slides themselves never use a finish). While a slide or Black is on a screen the device stays awake under **While a service is running**, and lets go when you clear it. A slide covers the countdown layouts (including Up next only) and the red overtime screen only while it is sent there; **Clear slide** brings the same layout back. In overtime the corner countdown becomes a red chip with the time over (+0:42).
  - Slides can be shown with no service running, and ending a service keeps everything stored. The library and the look are kept with the app's saved data and in backups (a backup with songs needs this version or later to open; one without is unchanged). Your picture stays on this device. A phone, tablet or web screen on an earlier version keeps showing the countdown.

## 0.37.0, 23/November/2026

- **The screen stays on only while a service is running.** The app used to keep the screen on whenever it was open, even when nothing was happening, which drained the battery. Now the screen is kept awake while a service or quick timer is running (paused too, and while counting down to a scheduled start), while this device is a stage screen, and while it is sharing to other screens or watching as a viewer. On Live standing by, and in Settings, Programs and Reports with nothing running, the screen can go to sleep as the phone normally would, and it is held again as soon as a service starts and let go as soon as it ends, with nothing to press. **Keep screen awake** in Settings now has three choices: **While a service is running** (the new standard), **Always** (the old behaviour: on whenever the app is open) and **Never**. A stage screen always stays on, whichever you choose. If you had switched the old setting off, it stays **Never**; everyone else is now on **While a service is running**. A web stage screen (opened in a browser) asks the browser to keep the screen on only while it is a stage display or a service is running, and lets go otherwise. On a PC the computer still does not go to sleep while a service runs or screens are shared, but the monitor may now turn off when no service is running. **Always** is kept on this phone or PC only, not in backups; the saved-state version is unchanged, so earlier versions keep working.
- **Send message no longer insists on text that fits the LED sign when no sign is in use.** The LED sign is 32 pixels wide, so a message such as "Thank you" held still (**Static**) is too wide for it, and the **Continue** button stayed off until you changed the effect, even when the message was only going to stage screens. The width check now applies only when the display in use is an LED sign (or the Simulator standing in for one). With stage screens, the second screen or the Stage simulator, any width can be sent, the effect list says **Static** and **Flash then hold** without "(must fit)", and a short note says if the text would be too wide for a sign. If you later switch to a sign, a message or cue that is too wide is refused with the same explanation as before, and nothing already saved is rejected or changed. The same applies to the stage cue editor, the end screen and zero screen editors, warning cues, the program editor and importing a program. The message a new user starts with, "Thank you" with **Scroll long text**, already fits the sign and is unchanged. The LED sign itself is unchanged.
- **Stage countdown layouts, with Up next only.** Stage screens now follow the theme chosen in Settings, and the countdown is restyled. **Timer only** has the digits as large as the screen allows; **Timer and segment name** adds a **NOW** band across the top (the name large, the speaker quieter); **Timer and up next** adds an **UP NEXT** band along the bottom (the name, then who has it and how long); a long name ends in **…** on a wide screen and wraps on a phone held upright. A new fourth choice, **Up next only**, shows just the next item (**UP NEXT**, its name large, who and how long) and no time at all; on the last segment, in a Quick timer or with no program it shows the end screen set for the program, or the clock where there is none. A line along the bottom edge shows how much of the segment has gone. It and the digits turn amber in the warning period (the warning times you already set), and a paused countdown is dimmed and says **PAUSED**. The digits keep a fixed width, so they do not shift as they count.
  - **Overtime is hard to miss.** In overtime the whole screen turns solid red with large dark digits counting up (+0:42) and **OVER TIME**, steady and never flashing. A new setting, **Overtime alert** in Settings › Display settings (next to At zero), sets how strongly: **Gentle** (the theme's background, red digits and a thick red frame), **Strong** (the red screen, the default) or **Strongest** (the red screen pulsing slowly about every two seconds; steady with Reduced motion). Up next only shows no time, so overtime does not change it. The setting is kept on this device, not in backups, and older saved data reads as Strong.
  - **Picture tiles for the layout.** Everywhere the layout is chosen (second screen, paired stage screens, web screens, full screen on a phone and the stage simulator) the choice is now four picture tiles, each a small drawing of the layout with its name and a tick on the one chosen. They wrap to two to a row on a phone and with large text.
  - Older paired stage screens do not know Up next only: they show the segment name under the timer. Web screens always match the app. The LED sign is unchanged.
- **A flip clock whenever a stage screen shows the time of day.** After **Show clock**, when a service or program ends on the clock (no end screen, including a Quick timer), and before a scheduled service, until the countdown to its start begins, stage screens now show a flip clock instead of plain digits: the hours and minutes on cards that fold over when a digit changes, with the weekday in the theme's colour and the date (for example *Monday*, *23 November 2026*) under them. It follows the **Clock format** setting: 12 hour has no leading zero (8:59) and AM and PM tiles with the current one lit, 24 hour has two digits (08:59) and no tiles. Held upright, a phone keeps the hours and minutes in one row with the AM and PM tiles in a row under them; on a wider screen the tiles stand beside the time. It follows the theme chosen in Settings, light themes included, and shows on the second screen, paired stage screens, web screens, full screen and the stage simulator. With **Reduced motion** the digits change without folding. A banner cue still shows over the clock. The LED sign and its simulator are unchanged. The host now also sends the day, the date and the theme's colours to stage screens; a stage screen or web screen on an older version still shows the plain clock, and a new screen with an older host shows the flip clock in white on black with its own day and date.
- **Finish: choose what the app is made of.** Settings › Appearance › Theme has a new **Finish** section. Your theme still chooses the colours; the finish chooses the material of the app's background and panels. Pick a picture tile to try it: the app behind the editor changes at once, and **Save appearance** keeps it. There are eleven: **Glass** (frosted panes over a soft glow), **Bridge console** (brushed steel plates with corner screws and keycap buttons), **Brick**, **Cashmere** (soft knit, no hard edges), **Denim** (twill with a stitched seam), **Plastic** (glossy moulded shells), **Walnut** and **Oak** (panels set into a dark or a pale wooden desk), **Leather** (stitched hide), **Marble** (polished stone with fine veins) and **Carbon fibre**, besides **Flat**, the plain panels you have always had, which stays the default: nothing changes until you choose one. **Strength** has **Subtle** (the default) and **Full**.
  - **Words always sit on a panel and stay easy to read.** The texture shows in the gaps between panels, at the edges and on the header, and is quiet under text. Each finish is tinted by your theme, built-in or custom, and is held back wherever it would make body text, grey text or the accent harder to read than the plain theme; pale themes get a paler material for that reason. A test checks this for every finish, strength and built-in theme, down to the pixels drawn.
  - **Where it does not apply.** **High contrast** turns every finish off, so outlines and text stay plain. Stage screens, the second screen, web screens, full screen, the stage picture in the simulator and the LED sign never use a finish. Nothing is sent to any other device.
  - **Paper & Ink has its own finish, Papery.** Whenever the theme is **Paper & Ink** or **Paper & Ink (blue)**, the app is drawn in **Papery**: a warm sheet of paper with a fine tooth, and brighter panels like loose sheets with a short soft shadow, a crisp thin edge and square printed buttons. It is part of those themes, not a choice, so it is not among the Finish tiles; with a Paper & Ink theme selected, the Finish section shows a short note in its place, and **Strength** (Subtle or Full) still applies. The finish you had chosen is kept underneath and comes back with any other theme. Custom themes are not Paper & Ink, even with the same colours. **High contrast** still draws plain panels.
  - **Saved with the look.** The finish and its strength are part of the appearance, so **Save look** keeps them and the default look returns to Flat. Like the rest of the appearance they stay on this device and are not in a backup, except inside a look you saved. Appearance saved before this version opens as Flat, Subtle. The materials are drawn by the app (no pictures are added to it) and drawn once, so a finish costs no extra work while a service runs.

## 0.36.0, 22/November/2026

- **The same text face on every PC.** The app carries the Roboto font, but its own text (buttons, headings, lists, dialogs) was drawn in Roboto only on Android. On Windows it used Segoe UI, whose letters are a different width, so a label that fitted in every test could wrap or be cut short on a PC, as **Custom** did in a narrow window. Every platform now draws the app's text in Roboto, as the stage screens, second screen and mini timer already did. Letters on a PC look a little different, and labels, headings and buttons now take the same room on a PC as on the Android screenshots the layouts are tested against. Nothing is saved or changed in programs, backups or settings.
- **A notice after an update points to what is new.** The first time the app opens on a newer version, Live shows a small notice while standing by: **Updated to 0.36.0**, with **See what’s new** (opens the What’s new page, after the Settings PIN if one is set) and **Dismiss**. It shows once per version: opening it or dismissing it marks that version as seen. A new install (no saved data yet, or data cleared) shows nothing and just notes the version; an install updated from a version before this notice shows it once for the first update; a downgrade shows nothing. It follows the same rules as the update notice: never during a service, quick timer, rehearsal, guided practice or Check this service, with the controls locked or while joined to another device's service, and never on a stage screen, the second screen or full screen. If a service is running when the app starts, the notice waits for the next time the app is standing by. When a newer version is also available, that notice shows first and this one after it is dismissed. The version last seen is kept on this device only, in the app's saved state (`seenVersion`): not in backups or program files, and a restored backup leaves it alone. The saved-state version is unchanged, so earlier versions simply ignore it. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Full screen follows the other landscape, even with auto-rotate off.** On a phone or tablet, full screen used to turn between the two landscapes only if the system's auto-rotate switch was on. It now follows the sensor between the two landscapes whatever that switch says, so turning the phone the other way round turns the stage with it. Leaving full screen puts the screen back exactly as the app had asked for it before. It needs no new permission, and Windows is unaffected.
- **On a phone or tablet, ending the service leaves full screen.** When the service ends, whether from End service in the tray, End service on Live, or from a paired device, full screen on a phone or tablet closes and the screen turns back to how it was before full screen, as in 0.34.0. A PC is unchanged: full screen stays open on the end screen, or the clock for a program with no end screen, as 0.35.0 describes, and closes when the next service is set up.
- **A one-time tip about the gestures.** The first time full screen opens on a device, a short overlay shows the three double-tap zones with their actions (left third **Undo**, middle **Pause or resume**, right third **Next segment**, each asking first) and how to bring up the control tray: swipe up from the bottom or tap once on a phone or tablet; click once or move the mouse to the bottom edge on a PC. It goes on a tap or click, any key, or after about 8 seconds, and does not come back by itself. The tap that closes it is never also taken as part of a double tap. A new **Show gestures** button in the control tray brings it back whenever you want. With the controls locked it does not show, so it never gets in the way of Hold to unlock, and it is not used up. With Reduced motion it does not fade. It is drawn on this screen only and is never sent to any stage screen. Whether it has been shown is kept on this phone or PC, not in backups or program files.
- **Send message, the end screen and the zero screen follow the simulator.** The Send message window below the stage cues always showed the LED sign, even with the Simulator set to Stage screen. It now has the same **LED sign | Stage screen** switch as the cue editor, starting on the display in use: Stage screen with the Stage simulator or stage screens, LED sign otherwise. Only the settings for the display you are looking at show: colour, position, speed and Wrap long text for Stage screen; display font, Tall, alignment and LED speed for LED sign. The text, Bold, Italic and Effect show on both. What is sent is the same as before, so a message set up on either side reaches the sign and screens exactly as it did, and older paired screens are unaffected.
  - The same switch is now on **Edit end screen** in Settings › Display settings, **At zero** in Settings › Display settings (Custom text), and the program editor's end screen, including Edit upcoming. They start on the display in use, and what they save is unchanged. Cues and warnings work as before. The Clock choice for **When the service ends** is untouched.
- **Check for updates ignores test versions.** A test version (one with a hyphen after its number, such as 0.36.0-beta.1) is never offered as an update, by Check for updates or the automatic check. A phone or PC running a test version is still offered the real version that follows it, for example 0.36.0 after 0.36.0-beta.1. A test version's number shows in full in About.
- **Classic fills the window and keeps the controls in view at any width.** In a window about 964 wide and 1100 high the timer card ended with a large empty area under it while the right column ran on with the display, the cues and the rundown, so the rundown sat far down the page. Classic now measures its cards and places them in two columns so that both end at about the same height: the rundown goes under the timer card when that fills the gap, and a card changes column when that is the only way to avoid a band of empty page. Windows from about 740 wide (820 when under 1000 high) use two columns; narrower ones use one. The ring and the controls are fitted to the window height in every width from 600 up, not only from 1000, so Pause, Next segment and End service stay in view without scrolling in a window 700 or more high; the ring still fills the card and keeps its size from Ready to running. **Custom**, **Pre-service countdown** and the other labels shrink a little in a tight space rather than breaking a word or ending in "…".
- **A sweep test checks Live at every width.** It runs Classic from 600 to 2000 wide in steps of 20, plus the window sizes reported, at heights from 600 to 1100, standing by, in Ready and running, and on tablets held both ways, and checks that nothing overflows, no label or heading is cut or wrapped, the controls are in view and no large empty band is left.
- **Ctrl+K and the other Ctrl keys keep working.** On a PC they could work once and then only now and then. Two things could cause that, and both are fixed: a key press the keyboard reported as a held-down repeat (after a key release went missing) was ignored, and the app's idea of which keys were held could go wrong after another window, such as the mini timer, had the keyboard. A press after a pause now always counts, and the held keys are checked again whenever the window changes. Ctrl+K and Ctrl+M also work if a text box had kept the keyboard.
- **The key line always shows ? and Ctrl K.** In a narrow window the line along the bottom of Live drops the least needed keys. **? Keys** and **Ctrl K Commands** now always stay, at the end, so you can always find the list of keys and the Commands list.

## 0.35.0, 21/November/2026

- **The Ready line no longer points at the wrong place.** With a pre-service start time set, the line under Ready said the display "follows the pre-service start time above", but in Mission control and some other layouts the pre-service countdown is not above it. It now reads "Timing has not started. The display shows the pre-service countdown until you start the first segment." in every layout.
- **The keys line shows on laptop screens too.** Three panes and The dial listed the keys along the bottom of the middle pane only in a window at least 800 high, so a 1366 × 768 laptop, or a 1920 × 1200 screen at 150% display scaling (about 1270 × 790), had none even with room for it. It now shows whenever key labels are on and the ring still has room above the controls with the line taken out; the ring gives up a little height for it. That is every common screen from 1280 × 720 up, to ultrawide and 4K, in Three panes, The dial, Mission control and Keys. A segment's private note takes one line under the title where a block of its own would leave the ring too little room, as in a 1366 × 768 window. Only a window 600 high, too short for the panes anyway, leaves the line out. Classic shows each key on its button, as before.
- **Classic looks right in a window under 1200 wide.** Such a window, for example a 1366 × 768 screen at 125% display scaling, a window not filling a 1920 × 1200 screen at 150%, or a 1024 × 768 screen, uses Classic whatever the layout chosen. There the controls beside a small ring were squeezed, so **Custom** broke over two lines, the display heading and its badge were cut short with "…", the cue buttons were narrow and a large empty band was left under the timer. Classic now puts the ring under the segment's name with Pause, Next segment, the time adjustments and End service across the card under it, or, in a shorter window, the segment's name beside the ring, whichever gives the larger ring; in Ready the form takes the place of the controls. The ring fills the height, so the card reaches down to the foot of the window, and the display and cues keep a column at least 340 wide. Wherever **Custom** has no room for its key beside it, the key is left out, so the word stays whole; Pause and Next do the same in the narrowest windows. The **DISPLAY PREVIEW** heading shortens to **PREVIEW** rather than either it or the display's badge being cut short, here and in the other layouts. Cue buttons are never so narrow that a word breaks across lines. A laptop window 1200 or more wide keeps the controls beside the ring, as before.
- **No end screen: show the clock.** A program can now end on the clock instead of an end screen. In the program editor the end screen card is now **When the service ends**, with a choice of **End screen** (the text and preview, as before) or **Clock**, which says "No end screen. When you end the service, the sign and stage screens show the clock." Choosing End screen again while the editor is open brings back the text you had. Settings › Display settings › **Default end screen** has the same choice for new programs and for quick timers; choosing End screen there again starts from "Hallelujah", ready to edit. Every program and the default keep their end screen, "Hallelujah" to begin with, until you choose Clock.
  - When a service with no end screen ends, the LED sign, paired phones and tablets, web screens, the second screen, full screen on the host, the stage simulator and the LED simulator all show the clock, in the 12 or 24-hour format chosen. Live says **Showing the clock**, Stage & display says **clock**, and End service asks to "show the clock" instead of "show “Hallelujah”" (and, with the LED sign, says the sign will show the clock). Blank screen, cues and messages after the end go back to the clock when they are cleared; **Show clock** is greyed out there, as the clock is already showing, and the button that brought back the end screen says **Back to clock**. Program details, Programs on a PC and the import review say **No end screen** and **Shows the clock when the service ends**.
  - A paired phone or tablet still on an earlier version shows the clock too. Programs, backups and program files you already have are unchanged and keep their end screens. A backup or program file with a program, or the default, set to Clock needs this version or later to open: earlier versions need an end screen and refuse it.
- **Check for updates automatically.** Settings › About has a new switch under Check for updates, **Check for updates automatically**, with the line "Once a day, when no service is running. Nothing is downloaded or installed without asking." It is off to begin with. When it is on, the app asks GitHub for the latest version in the same way as Check for updates, at most once a day: about 30 seconds after the app opens, and again when the app comes back to standing by. It never asks during a service, a quick timer, a rehearsal, guided practice or Check this service, or while the controls are locked. When a newer version is published, Live shows a small notice while standing by, **Version X is available**, with **See update**, which opens Check for updates (after the Settings PIN, if one is set), and **Not now**, which hides it for that version; a newer one shows it again. The notice is never shown during a service, on a stage screen, the second screen or full screen. If the check cannot reach GitHub it says nothing and tries again the next day; pressing Check for updates still says when it could not check. The switch and what the check found are kept on this phone or PC, not in backups or program files. It works on Android and Windows. Settings › About › Your data now says the automatic check, when switched on, contacts GitHub once a day.
- **Ready shows the timer you will have.** On a PC, a service loaded but not yet started showed the same small ring in every desktop layout, and the layout's own timer only appeared once the first segment started. Ready now shows each layout's own timer, the same size and in the same place it will be when the service starts, with the first segment's full time and not counting. The Ready form takes the place of the timer controls: in **Three panes** and **The dial**, the program with **Change**, **Simulator · all set**, the pre-service countdown, the service mode, the line saying timing has not started, and **Start first segment**, **Cancel setup** and **Check this service** sit two to a row under the ring, where Pause and Next segment go. **The dial** shows its service ring with the first segment marked. In **Mission control** the strip across the top says Ready, with the readiness row, the line and the three buttons where End service goes, and the rest of the form is under the dial; in a window under 760 high the program and the service mode sit beside the dial instead of its figures. **Keys** shows its own countdown strip with Start first segment, Cancel setup and Check this service where Pause and Next go, and the form under the rundown, which says Ready for the first segment. Classic already used the same ring. Starting the service still asks first, as before, and nothing about timing changes.
- **The ring fills the middle pane at every size.** In Three panes and The dial the ring used to stay at its laptop size until the window reached 1920 × 1080, leaving empty space above and below it on a laptop or a PC with display scaling. It now grows with the height of the pane at every size, as large as fits while the segment name, Pause, Next segment, the time adjustments and End service stay in view without scrolling; in a 1920 × 1200 window at 150% (about 1270 × 790) it is about a third larger. Mission control's dial grows into spare height in the same way, and Ready keeps room for the keys line along the bottom so the ring does not change size when the service starts. In a very short window Three panes puts a segment's private note on one line under the title, as The dial already did.
- **Classic fits a laptop window.** In a wide window less than 1000 high, such as a 1366 × 768 laptop or one at 125% display scaling (1093 × 614, which is too narrow for the desktop layouts, so it uses Classic whatever the choice), Classic now puts the controls beside the ring, so Pause, Next, the time adjustments and End service, or the Ready form and Start first segment, are in view without scrolling. Larger windows, phones and tablets in portrait look as before.
- The window sizes Windows display scaling gives the app (1280 × 720, 1536 × 864, 1270 × 790, 1440 × 900 and 1093 × 614) are now checked in every layout, running and in Ready, alongside 1200 × 600, 1366 × 768, 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440. A window 600 high is still too short for the panes of Three panes, The dial and Mission control to show every control without scrolling.
- **Release notes say how to install on Windows.** Below each release's notes, the install lines now cover the Windows installer as well as the Android app: which file to download, what to do if Windows says "Windows protected your PC", and that a PC on 0.34.0 or later can use **Download and install** instead. The checksum line says it is the APK's; the Windows files keep their own `.sha256` files.
- **The time on screens holds still, before the service too.** The countdown to a scheduled service's start, and the clock shown before it, still moved and changed size slightly each second on the stage simulator, the copy of the second screen on Live, the second screen itself, full screen on the host and paired phones and tablets, because they were drawn in the device's own font (Segoe UI on a PC) and refitted to the screen each time they changed. They are now drawn like the countdown during a service. On all of these, and on web screens, every digit now has a place as wide as the widest digit, so the time keeps one size and each digit keeps its place whatever the font, during the service and before it. The time changes size only when it gains or loses a character, such as the + of overtime, or the screen changes size. The clock's AM and PM take the same room too, and on web screens a 12-hour clock now stays on one line instead of putting AM or PM on a second line in some browsers.
  - Everything on these screens is now in Roboto, the font the app carries, on a PC too: cues, banners, the segment name and up next line, and the second screen's own window, which drew some of them in Windows' own font (Segoe UI). The large time in the mini timer on a PC is in Roboto too. The rest of the app on a PC keeps Windows' font. Web screens still use the browser's own font, as the app does not send one to them.
- **Gestures and a control tray in full screen.** On the stage in full screen, double-tap or double-click the right third of the screen to go to the next segment, the middle to pause or resume, and the left third to undo the last segment change. Each asks first, in the same small card as the keys and buttons; with nothing to undo, a short "Nothing to undo" shows and fades. A single tap or click, a swipe up from the bottom edge, or moving the mouse to the bottom of the screen brings up a small tray along the bottom with Pause or Resume, Next, −2 min, +2 min, Undo, Cues, Show clock, Blank screen, End service and Exit; every timer action in it still asks first, and End service asks just as it does on Live. After End service, full screen stays open on the end screen; the timer controls grey out, while Cues, Clock, Blank and Exit still work. Full screen now stays on the end screen however the service ends, and closes when the next service is set up. The tray goes after six seconds untouched, and stays while the mouse is on it or a question from it is open; Esc, Back or a swipe down puts it away. A single tap or click waits a moment, so a double tap is not taken for it. The keys work as before. Nothing of this is sent to any stage screen.
